Search Console now holds a full quarter of measured data covering the period before, during and after the August fixes, and it says the fixes paid. The same data contradicts an open piece of work: the remaining prune list still contains pages carrying 15.8% of the site's total visibility at an average position of 7.6. That list was compiled from Ahrefs traffic estimates before Search Console access existed, and it should not be executed as written.

Why Ahrefs shows the opposite

The Ahrefs organic traffic estimate for the same period falls — 6,271 a month a year ago against 3,360 now. That number is modelled from ranking positions, not measured. It drops when pages leave the index, which is exactly what the consolidation did on purpose. Search Console counts actual clicks. Where the two disagree, the measured number is the one to report.

Stop before executing the rest of the prune list

11 of the 210 URLs still queued for removal are among the 25 most-seen pages on the site.

171,749 impressions — 15.8% of all site visibility — sit on pages marked for deletion

Their average position is 7.6: page one. The prune list was compiled from Ahrefs zero-traffic modelling before Search Console access existed, so pages that rank and are seen but convert poorly were indistinguishable from pages nobody sees at all. They are not the same thing, and the remedy for each is opposite: one gets deleted, the other gets a better title and a stronger reason to click.

This is not hypothetical. One has already gone: /despite-the-rise-of-e-commerce-physical-stores-are-here-to-stay/ carried 14,756 impressions at position 6.0 and now 301s to /company/ — a corporate about page with no topical relationship to what people were searching for. A redirect to an unrelated destination is treated as a soft removal, so that visibility is not preserved, it is discarded.

Recommendation. Freeze the remaining 210 URLs and re-screen them against Search Console impressions before any further deletion. The screen is cheap: any URL above roughly 500 impressions in 90 days is a rewrite candidate, not a deletion candidate. The genuinely dead pages — old press releases, event notices, duplicated service copy — can still go.

Why the CTR is 0.4%, and why it is mostly not a titles problem

The titles on the ten biggest pages were re-fetched live today and checked one by one.

Nine of the ten highest-impression pages carry a well-formed, length-appropriate title and a written meta description — the August repair held. So a 0.07% click-through rate at position 6.6, roughly fifty times below a normal page-one rate, has to come from somewhere else. It does: from what is being searched.

What this means for the KPI

A large share of the impression pool is generated on surfaces that do not produce clicks by design: an AI answer resolves the question in place. Chasing site-wide CTR back to an industry benchmark of 3–5% is not achievable while that pool keeps growing, and a falling CTR could even mean the site is being cited more. Clicks and non-branded clicks are the honest KPIs; site-wide CTR belongs in the report as a diagnostic, not a target. The corollary is that being the source an AI answer cites is now worth optimising for directly — the site already appears in those results.

There is still a real, recoverable click gap underneath this. The 25 highest-impression pages hold 383,864 impressions — 35.3% of all site visibility — and convert at 0.241%. Bringing just those 25 to a modest 1.0% would add roughly 2,900 clicks a quarter, a 66% increase in total site traffic, without a single new page being written.

The largest unowned opportunity is pop-up and experiential retail

Bigger, in measured impressions, than merchandising or brand ambassadors.

Five pages rank for pop-up and experiential retail queries. Together they take 79,416 impressions and 84 clicks — 7.3% of the site's visibility for 1.9% of its clicks. Four of the five are on the prune list. No service page owns the category: /pop-up-store-services/ exists but sits at position 9.4 with 4,004 impressions and seven clicks, and it is itself queued for deletion.

This is the pattern /retail-marketing-services/ and /retail-management-services/ were published to address in August: a category with real demand, page-one presence, and no URL claiming it. The difference is that this one is already ranking — the work is consolidation and a stronger hub, not new content from zero. It deserves testing before another merchandising or brand-ambassador page is written; those terms already have 7 and 15 competing URLs earning almost nothing.

Referring domains peaked at 3,654 in July and have fallen to 3,291 — a 9.9% decline, still running at roughly seven lost domains a day through early September. Domain Rating has not moved (43), which is the expected signature of low-quality links expiring: volume falls, authority does not. This is consistent with the troc* network previously identified as SEOExpress.org spam, which supplied 84% of anchor text. It is dying without intervention.

The drafted disavow file remains justified — the point is to make the removal deliberate and permanent rather than dependent on those domains staying expired — but it is no longer urgent, and the falling refdomain count should not be read as a loss of legitimate links.
