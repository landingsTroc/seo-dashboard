Executive summary

Three findings

Clicks / month 1,323 measured, not estimated
Impressions / quarter 1.21M we are seen constantly
Click-through rate 0.33% 2–5% is normal
Non-branded clicks 16.9% 248 clicks in 90 days

1. The content consolidation worked. It removed 279 pages that were being shown in search results and never clicked. Impressions fell 43%, but clicks fell only 13% — so click-through rate rose by half. This should be reported as a completed win.

2. The real problem is that we are almost never clicked. Google showed trocglobal.com 1.21 million times last quarter and sent 3,969 visits. That is a 0.33% click-through rate against a 2–5% norm. Twenty-five pages rank on page one with a CTR below 2%. The traffic problem is not ranking — it is conversion from ranking.

3. Almost all of it is our own name. Of sampled clicks, 83.1% are branded. In 90 days, just 248 clicks came from people who did not already know T-ROC.

Why the numbers differ from previous reports

Earlier reports used Ahrefs, which estimates traffic from rankings. Search Console counts clicks. For this site Ahrefs overstates by 3.8× — it reported 5,008 monthly visits where Google measured 1,323. Every prior figure sourced from Ahrefs was inflated by roughly that factor, and all targets here have been rebased. Ahrefs remains reliable for rankings, backlinks and Domain Rating; it is not reliable for traffic.

The July spike is the clearest evidence we have

In July, Ahrefs recorded the site's highest-ever ranking counts — 519 keywords and 235 in the Top 3. Search Console recorded that same month's lowest click-through rate of the quarter (0.23%). More top rankings, fewer clicks per impression. Rankings are not the constraint.

Do not report the backlink growth as a win

Backlinks rose 25.9% in two months, but the growth is not earned. An external spam network is generating links using domains built to mimic ours — 84% of inbound link text is spam, advertising an SEO reseller. Referring-domain and backlink counts are inflated by it, and filing a disavow is a cleanup item in Phase 4, not a growth lever.

25 pages hold a top-10 position with CTR under 2% — ten to a hundred times below expectation. Those six alone drew 221,000 impressions and 349 clicks.

The size of the prize — and what we do not yet know

At an ordinary 3% CTR, those six pages would return roughly 6,900 clicks instead of 349. Site-wide, 1.21M impressions at a modest 2% would mean ~24,000 clicks a quarter against today's 3,969.

That is the scale of the opportunity, not a forecast. We have one verified cause and two unverified ones. Broken titles are confirmed (below). But 0.05% at position 5 is too extreme for titles alone — the other candidates are queries where our page is not what the searcher wanted, and AI Overviews answering before the click. Separating them needs a per-page query breakdown, which is the first task in Phase 1.

Roadmap

Four phases

Reordered around measured evidence. Click-through rate is now the primary metric: it is measured weekly, it moves fast, and it is the binding constraint on every other number.

PHASE 1

Fix the click-through rate

Highest return Weeks 1–3
  • Run the per-page query breakdown first. For the 25 low-CTR pages, identify which searches drive their impressions. This separates a title problem from an intent mismatch and decides whether we rewrite or retarget. No effort should be committed before this.
  • Repair the title system — remove the 50-character truncation and the duplicated brand suffix.
  • Rewrite the 12 truncated and 3 empty titles, each carrying its target search term.
  • Activate the H1 fix in WPCode — written since July 15, five minutes to deploy.
  • Measure weekly. CTR responds within days of re-crawl, so this validates or disproves itself quickly.
PHASE 2

Convert the two head terms

Weeks 2–5
  • "brand ambassador" — 3,755 impressions at position 10.8. Consolidate the ~12 competing articles into the service page so one strong page ranks instead of a dozen weak ones.
  • "retail merchandising services" — 3,735 impressions at position 10.5, pointing at a money page.
  • Internal links from the highest-authority pages to both targets.
  • Moving either from position 10 to position 3–5 changes them from ~8 clicks a quarter to several hundred.
PHASE 3

Finish the cleanup and unblock crawling

Weeks 1–6
  • Cloudflare / WP Engine: allow Googlebot, Bingbot and AhrefsBot. Requires the infrastructure administrator.
  • Complete the consolidation — 210 of 489 flagged zero-traffic pages are still live and still being crawled.
  • Attack the 465 "crawled, not indexed" — the largest not-indexed bucket. Finishing the prune is the direct treatment; anything remaining after that is a content-quality problem needing consolidation or improvement, not more pages.
  • Trace the 90 404s. They are not from the prune list, so the source is unknown — likely broken internal links or stale external references.
  • Audit the 64 noindex pages and the 21 where Google overrode our canonical.
  • Target: indexation rate from 39.6% back above 55%.
PHASE 4

Break the brand dependence

Weeks 4–12
  • Build on whatever Phase 1 proves: if titles drive the CTR gap, apply the fix site-wide; if intent mismatch does, retarget the affected pages.
  • Optimise for AI answers — the definitional queries losing clicks are exactly what AI Overviews absorb.
  • File the disavow for the spam link network. Hygiene, not growth — Google already discounts most of it.
  • Target: non-branded click share from 16.9% to 30%.

The one thing holding up the highest-return work

Phase 1 needs WPCode access on production. Editing the SEO plugin fields directly will not hold — an override snippet overwrites them. Without that access the corrected titles can only be delivered as a specification for someone else to apply.

Measurement. Click, impression, CTR and position figures come from the Google Search Console API for the property https://trocglobal.com/, May 4 – August 2, 2026. Search Console data lags 2–3 days by Google's design. The property is URL-prefix, so it excludes jobs.trocglobal.com.

On Ahrefs. Ahrefs figures appear only for rankings, backlinks and Domain Rating. Its traffic estimates overstate measured clicks by 3.8× for this site and are not used for any target here. Its keyword coverage is the top 100 by traffic; the plan in use ignores pagination, so the long tail is unmeasured.

Branded classification is by explicit pattern match on T-ROC's names. Ahrefs' automatic flag is unreliable for this domain — it marks "walmart wallpaper" as branded and "troc" as not.
