The July anomaly is the most important number on this page

In July the site held its highest-ever ranking counts — 519 keywords and 235 in the Top 3 — and its lowest traffic in twelve months (3,290). More top rankings produced less traffic. That combination is not normal, and it is the single strongest piece of evidence that our rankings are not converting into clicks. The root cause is identified in the next section.

Search Console contradicts the Ahrefs rebound — read this before acting on the numbers above

Google Search Console data through July 23 shows impressions down 51.8% — from a June daily average of 16,051 to 7,738 — with no recovery in the data available. Over the same window Google dropped 127 pages out of the index (821 → 694), in two steps on June 30 and July 10.

Important context before reading this as damage: all 489 pruned pages had zero traffic, and 279 of them now redirect correctly with no broken links or redirect chains. Removing zero-traffic pages is supposed to reduce impressions — those pages were appearing in search results without ever earning a click. Much of this decline may be the intended effect working as designed.

The decisive number is clicks, which this export does not contain. If clicks held steady while impressions halved, the consolidation succeeded and click-through rate roughly doubled. If clicks fell in proportion, real traffic was lost. One Search Console export settles it.

Until then, treat both the Ahrefs rebound and this impressions decline as unconfirmed, and do not present either as the outcome of the consolidation.

One caveat on the backlink growth

Backlinks rose 26% in two months, but this is not a win. An external spam network is generating links using domains that mimic ours. 84% of incoming link text is spam. The growth is not earned and should not be reported as an achievement — it is a cleanup item, covered in Phase 4.

The cause: our page titles are cut off in Google

The system that generates the site's titles truncates them at 50 characters and then appends - T-ROC. The result is a title that reads as broken at the exact moment a searcher decides whether to click.

Verification required before we invest

This diagnosis rests on Ahrefs data, which estimates traffic using a model. Before committing significant effort we will confirm it against Google Search Console, which reports actual clicks. High impressions with near-zero clicks confirms the diagnosis; low impressions would mean the ranking data is stale and priorities change.

Roadmap

Four-phase execution plan

Phases are ordered by dependency: Phase 0 unblocks everything else, and Phase 1 carries the highest return per hour of effort.

PHASE 0

Unblock

Blocks everything else Week 1
  • Cloudflare / WP Engine: allow Googlebot, Bingbot and AhrefsBot; review rate-limiting rules. Requires the infrastructure administrator.
  • Activate the H1 fix in WPCode — the code is already written.
  • Lower the Ahrefs crawl rate to clear false errors from the Health Score.
  • Validate the title diagnosis against real Search Console click data.
  • Diagnose the 127 de-indexed pages — identify which URLs left the index on June 30 and July 10, and whether the content consolidation removed pages that were still earning traffic.
PHASE 0.5

Recover the index

New — supersedes Phase 1 in priority Weeks 1–3
  • Finish the consolidation — it is only 57% done. Of 489 pages marked for pruning, 279 were redirected but 210 are still live. These are zero-traffic pages still being crawled, and they are the most likely source of the "crawled, not indexed" backlog.
  • 465 pages "crawled, currently not indexed" — Google saw them and declined. The largest single bucket, and a content-quality signal rather than a technical fault.
  • 90 pages returning 404 — these are not from the prune list, which was verified clean with zero 404s. Their source needs identifying.
  • 21 pages where Google chose a different canonical than we specified.
  • Re-submit for validation and monitor the indexed count back toward its 821 peak.
PHASE 1

Repair the title layer

Highest return Weeks 1–2
  • Fix the system that truncates titles at 50 characters and eliminate the duplicated brand suffix.
  • Rewrite the 12 truncated titles, incorporating each page's target search term.
  • Fill the 3 empty titles and rewrite descriptions across the merchandising cluster.
  • Estimated return: ≈ +500 visits/month (+10% of total traffic), with no new content created.
PHASE 2

Convert near-miss rankings

Weeks 2–4
  • “retail customer experience” (2,900/mo, position #4) is currently answered by the wrong article. The largest single opportunity on the site.
  • Zero-difficulty terms sitting at positions 11–12 that need only title and internal-link adjustments.
  • Rebuild internal links to pages orphaned by the content cleanup.
PHASE 3

Reduce brand dependence

Weeks 4–8
  • Consolidate the brand-ambassador articles to push the service page toward the Top 10 on an 18,000-searches/month term.
  • Complete the cleanup of non-performing content.
  • Target: lift non-branded traffic from 22% to 35%.
PHASE 4

Hygiene and defense

Ongoing
  • File the disavow of spam links with Google. This is preventive hygiene, not growth — Google already discounts most of these links.
  • Image alt text, missing descriptions, and JavaScript errors.
  • AI answer optimization: the search terms that lost traffic are definitional — precisely the queries absorbed by Google's AI-generated summaries.

Decision point

Without Search Console and WPCode access, Phase 1 — the highest-return work — cannot be executed. The infrastructure fixes requested on July 15 remain open and are holding back the recovery.

Methodology. Ahrefs traffic figures are modeled estimates, not measured clicks; Search Console and GA4 are the source of truth and will be integrated to close the measurement loop. All month-over-month comparisons use snapshots taken on the 5th of each month at identical scope settings.

Keyword analysis covers the top 100 of 429 terms (≈91% of traffic); the long tail is unexamined. The branded/non-branded split was classified manually, as Ahrefs' automatic classification proved unreliable for this domain.

The 21% year-over-year decline has several simultaneous causes — broken titles, blocked crawlers, content consolidation and pressure from AI-generated answers — and should not be attributed to any single one.
