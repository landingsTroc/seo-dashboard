Broken redirect chains repaired

Six URLs were returning 301 into a 404 — a rule actively feeding crawlers dead pages. All six now resolve in a single hop, and every internal link pointing at them was repaired at source so the crawler stops rediscovering them. One repair collided with a redirect rule the editor account could not see and briefly created a loop on two URLs; it was caught and cleared in the same session by aligning the post slug to the rule that already existed.

Internal links pointing at redirects: 103 repaired across 75 records

Twenty-six verified mappings, each destination confirmed to return 200 in one hop before any edit. Applied to both the post content and the Elementor layout data, with a backup of both fields per record and a read-back check after every write. 75 of 75 verified, zero failures.

Seven orphaned commercial pages connected

Measured against the site’s real link graph rather than the crawl: 26 pages had no contextual link from any post or page body. The seven with commercial value — the three competitor comparisons, wireless retail services, pop-up store services, experiential retail strategy and the product launch playbook — now have contextual links from topically relevant pages.

Forty Google-search links unwrapped

Forty-eight links across eleven posts pointed at google.com/search?q=… with the real destination buried in the query string. Forty now point at the destination directly. Seventeen of the twenty-five internal destinations were dead, so a plain unwrap would have turned them into broken links; each was mapped to a verified live equivalent instead.

One SERP title shortened

/retail-staffing-services/ went from 75 to 55 characters. Two records claim that URL: the page serves it and held the long title, while the invisible post already had a good short one. They now match.

One mapping was nearly a mistake

/what-we-do/performance/ redirects to /services/, which is the blocked page below. Unwrapping it without checking the final status would have created fresh links into a dead end. It was mapped to /solutions/ instead. Every destination in this report was checked by following the redirect to its end, not by reading the first response.

What the crawls actually showed

Three reports this fortnight described a larger problem than exists, or the wrong problem entirely.

“62 pages missing a meta description” is really nine

Checked record by record through the API: only nine lack a stored description, and eight of those are duplicate or empty records. Around 32 of the flagged URLs have descriptions that are simply never printed. The rest are thin taxonomy archives whose descriptions are not reachable through the API at all.

117 URLs are not served by WordPress

All 34 case studies, 79 videos and 4 ebooks are rendered by Webflow under the WordPress domain. Their HTML opens with data-wf-site and carries no Yoast output, which is why they have no meta description, no canonical and no schema. Their last Webflow publish was 30 December 2025.

The redirect-link report is mostly one header

Around 400 pages were flagged for linking to redirects. After the content sweep above, every page carries exactly four such links, identical on all of them: a navigation item, an inline script targeting it, the header “Schedule a Meeting” button and a footer call to action. That is the theme template, not content, and it was left untouched.

On those citations

They sit behind claims such as “80% of shoppers” and “the cost of a bad hire”, wrapped in a Google search so a click never exposes the 404. HBR, NRF, NielsenIQ, Shopify, RetailDive and Psychology Today all return 404 for the exact URLs used. They were left in place rather than quietly deleted, because removing a citation changes what the article claims. The choice is to drop the claim or find a real source.
